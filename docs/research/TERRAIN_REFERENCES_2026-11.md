# Terrain references: the Pannonian plain in the mid-1980s

Research for the map's look: reference photographs, landscape traits and fixed camera views for
the Great Hungarian Plain ("Pannonia") as it was around the war's date, 15 October 1985, with
summer missions in mind too. Compiled 3 November 2026.

## Method

- **Sources.** Wikimedia Commons (searched through its API by category and by search;
  licence, date, author and description read from each file's `extmetadata`) and Fortepan.
  Fortepan photos were reached through their Commons copies, which keep the Fortepan number
  in the title, so every Fortepan entry gives both the Fortepan page
  (`https://fortepan.hu/hu/photos/?id=N`) and the Commons file page. Fortepan's own site
  is drawn by JavaScript and its search API could not be used in this session.
- **Links only.** No image was downloaded or copied. Each entry is a link to the page that
  holds the photo.
- **Verified.** On 3 November 2026 the Commons API confirmed that all 70 chosen files and
  the 19 further candidates exist, and gave each one's licence, date and author. Where a
  file's description does not say what is in the frame, the "shows" column says so, or it
  names the Commons category the file is filed under. Those entries should be looked at on
  their page before modelling from them.
- **Period.** Photos from 1931-1986 show the period itself: Bazard's 1974 colour road
  trip, Fortepan's MHSZ aerials of 1975-1984 and the 1983 Csanádapáca series, and Blobelt's
  1986 roll film. Modern photos are used only for things that have hardly changed: field
  soil, crops, reeds, oxbows, beech and oak forest, shadoofs, concrete water towers and
  silos of the 1960s-80s, pylons, and concrete and wooden poles. Each modern photo's notes
  say what to leave out, such as phone masts or later paint.

## Landscape traits (with sources)

T1. **Flat to rolling, huge, low.** The Great Hungarian Plain covers about 52,000 km², 56% of
Hungary. Its relief is "flat to rolling", and its highest point is Hoportyó at 183 m. The
horizon is therefore made of trees, towers and spires, not of hills, except on the northern
edge.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hungarian_Plain

T2. **Big collective fields.** Under the production systems of the 1970s-80s, the fields of a
Hajdúböszörmény cooperative were generally 100-200 ha. The small groves between them were
cleared. Cooperatives grew by merger: their area rose 88%, and the number of cooperatives
under 1,500 ha fell from 1,010 to 82 during the 1970s.
Sources: Erdészeti Lapok 1986/6 pp. 253-254,
https://erdeszetilapok.oszk.hu/00821/pdf/EL_1986_06_253-254.pdf ; Statisztikai Szemle 1984,
https://real-j.mtak.hu/22079/8/Statisztikai_Szemle_1984_62_08-09_.pdf.pdf
For the project: `data/scenery/map_dressing.json` has parcels of 250-700 m by 80-250 m
(about 2-17 ha). That is far smaller than 100-200 ha (for example 1,000 m by 1,500 m), and
should be reconsidered for the TSZ land. Small parcels remain right for the household plots
(háztáji) at the village edge.

T3. **Tree rows on the farm tracks, not belts on every field.** The 1950s field-protection
belts were planted mainly to shelter pastures. Later planting moved to tree rows along the
farm tracks (dűlőút). Black locust rows were cut back repeatedly and then renewed. Farmyards
(majors) were planted from 1963 with hybrid poplar and black locust, and pedunculate oak
later took the lead. A Simonii poplar row lined a farm's access road, and a pig farm was
reached by a 350 m paved access road.
Source: https://erdeszetilapok.oszk.hu/00821/pdf/EL_1986_06_253-254.pdf

T4. **Black locust everywhere, pine on the sand.** Black locust (akác) covers about 22.5% of
Hungary's forest area (2004). It grows 12-20 m tall, and its leaves start colouring on
average around 24 September, so it is turning in mid-October. The small woods of the
Nyírség plain are 1-3 ha fragments, mostly black locust, with honey-locust edges and elder
beneath. The Alföld's sand was afforested with black locust and black pine, and
field-protection belts (mezővédő erdősávok) were planted.
Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fehér_akác ;
https://epa.hu/01600/01614/00019/pdf/EPA01614_jame_2010_375-386.pdf

T5. **Tanyas in their tree islands.** Single farmsteads stand out on the open plain, each in
its own clump of trees. Bazard (1974) describes one as "dans son îlot d'arbres", and photos
P18 and P19 show two, one with a sweep well.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Une_ferme_entre_Debrecen_et_Szeghalom_(1).jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ferme_près_de_Bugac.jpg

T6. **Street villages on strip plots.** Plots are narrow and long. The house stands at the
street end, on the plot's side boundary, with its gable to the street. Farm buildings follow
behind it, and the garden or orchard is at the back. The 1982 aerials (Fortepan 84605,
84606) show this as it was in the period.
Sources: Magyar Néprajzi Lexikon, https://mek.oszk.hu/02100/02115/html/4-1459.html ;
https://fortepan.hu/hu/photos/?id=84605

T7. **The Kádár-kocka is the 1960s-80s house.** It is about 10 × 10 m (8 × 8 to 11 × 11),
about 100 m², with a pyramid (tent) roof and B30 brick walls. The render is often two
colours, and the windows are three-leaf wooden ones. It is set back behind a front garden.
More than 800,000 were built between 1959 and 1980, so by 1985 they stand among the older
gable-end long houses in every village.
Source: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kádár-kocka

T8. **Village streets.** Asphalt was laid on village side streets in the late 1970s and early
1980s, only 2.8-3.2 m wide (main streets 3.5-4.5 m). The roadside ditches are earth-bedded
soakaways. Grass verges sit between the ditches and the fences.
Source: Tiszacsege street works description,
https://tiszacsege.hu/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/A-1-Muszaki-leiras-Tiszacsege-MFP-2022-burkolatfelujitas-1.pdf

T9. **Poles and wires.** Low-voltage lines run on poles of wood, concrete or lattice steel,
7-12 m tall, with spans of about 35-40 m. The prestressed concrete poles have perforated
webs (the B12 type). Commons holds separate categories of concrete and of wooden utility
poles in Hungary, P39 is filed under the concrete ones and P31, P60 and P69
under the wooden ones.
Source: https://english.nmhh.hu/document/215849/8_Eon_prezentci_NMHH_ptetti_frum_v1.pdf

T10. **The shadoof (gémeskút).** Its parts are the forked post (ágas), the sweep (gém), the
well pole (kútostor) and the counterweight (kolonc). It was the most common water-lifting
device on the Alföld until a few decades ago, so it stands at tanyas and on pastures in 1985.
Source: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gémeskút

T11. **October ground.** In mid-October maize is mostly harvested: about 87% was in by 16
October in the reported year. The sunflower harvest starts in the second week of September,
and winter wheat is sown in October. The October plain is therefore a patchwork of fresh
brown ploughland, harrowed and sown ground with a faint green, maize stubble with stalk
bales, and a few standing maize fields. These calendar figures are from recent years, and
the 1985 dates may differ by a week or two.
Sources: https://trademagazin.hu/en/keszul-a-vetomagszektor-az-oszi-kalaszosok-szeptemberben-kezdodo-vetesere ;
https://agroberichtenbuitenland.nl/actueel/nieuws/2018/10/29/harvest-in-hungary
Photos: P03 (8 Oct 2006, ploughed), P10 (3 Oct, maize bales), P08 (maize harvest).

T12. **October weather and air.** Budapest in October has a mean of 12.7 °C, 141 hours of
sun and 39 mm of rain. The plain gets 1,700-2,100 hours of sun a year and is "very dusty in
summer". Autumn night fog on the Alföld lingers longest in the east and north-east (weak
source, forecasts only; no fog-day statistics found). Photo P12 shows how fog swallows the
far ground.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Hungary ;
https://infostart.hu/belfold/2024/12/01/marad-a-nyugodt-oszi-ido

T13. **Concrete landmarks.** Every town and many villages have a reinforced-concrete water
tower from the 1960s-70s. Examples: Körmend 1971, 600 m³, 44.3 m tall; Pápa, 41.5 m,
1,500 m³, on an 8.7 m base; Törökszentmiklós 1977; mushroom towers of the 1970s-80s.
Concrete grain silos stand at the stations, and farm dryers at the majors. Together with the
church spires, these are what a crew sees above the tree lines.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bercsényi_Miklós_Straße,_Wasserturm,_2023_Körmend.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_tower,_Bástya_Street,_2020_Pápa.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Táncsics_Mihály_Straße_Wasserturm,_2023_Törökszentmiklós.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pilzförmiger_Wasserturm,_2024_Szécsény.jpg

T14. **Rivers between dykes.** The Tisza and the Körös flow between flood dykes, with
floodplain forest and planted stands inside the dykes. Oxbows and dead arms (holtág, morotva)
carry reed beds. Dyke-crest roads run to the pumping houses.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ártéri_erdő_a_Tisza_mentén_Szeged_és_Algyő_között.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Szentes-Magyartés_Körös_folyó_elhagyott_medre.JPG ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dykes_in_Mindszent.jpg

T15. **The northern hills.** Mountain beech forest (Aconito-Fagetum) covers the Kékes plateau
of the Mátra. Lower slopes carry oak forest with steppe grassland (Stipa) on the open
south-facing slopes, as at Somlyó by Apc. From the plain, the Cserhát and the Mátra are a low
wooded wall behind the fields.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kékesi_bükkös.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Somlyóberg,_Wald,_Steppe,_2026_Apc.jpg ;
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Acker_und_Cserhát,_2023_Tar.jpg

T16. **Air-defence sites.** At its 1986-89 peak, Hungary's strategic air defence had 20
operational sites: the S-75M Volkhov (1977-95), the S-125M (1978-95) and one S-200VE site.
Eight Soviet S-125 units sat at airfields. The army's mobile air defence used the Kub (P68,
P70). No Hungarian Osa unit was found. The Fót battery (MN3121 "Búza", 11/8) was active
1981-1996.
Sources: https://geimint.blogspot.com/2008/10/hungarian-strategic-air-defense-cold.html ;
https://worldwidepanorama.org/wwp610/BanfalvyZoltan-6177.html

## Gaps

- No photo was found, on Commons or in Fortepan's Commons copies, of a Hungarian
  field-protection belt or a black-locust row along a farm track. These are the plain's most
  characteristic tree lines. Look for one in fentrol.hu frames or Fortepan's own search.
- No concrete panel fence (the usual 1980s factory and farmyard fence) was found with an
  open licence.
- Few photos show wooden telephone poles carrying many wires. Only photos filed under the
  wooden-pole category were found (P31, P60, P69), and none is from the 1980s.
- No ground photo of a 1980s industrial hall or TSZ machine hall was found, apart from
  Fortepan 70386 (1975) and the 1984 aerial of the Békéscsaba cannery.
- No openly licensed photo of a Warsaw Pact SAM site in 1980s Hungary. The leads are W01 and
  W02, which are for looking only. No Hungarian Osa unit was found: Hungary's army used the
  Kub.
- Fortepan's own search could not be used in this session. Its catalogue has far more 1980s
  village, road and farm photos than its Commons copies. Search fortepan.hu by place and year
  (1980-1986) for the field-edge, farmyard and road views.
- The web search budget for this session ran out before the Fortepan, Europeana and
  aerial-archive searches were finished.
- No fog or haze statistics (days with fog per month on the Alföld) were found. Only weather
  forecasts say where fog lingers.
- No written source was found for soil colour (chernozem and meadow soils, sand in the
  Kiskunság and Nyírség). It is only seen in the photos.
- Hungarian road cross-section standards of the 1980s (carriageway and shoulder widths for
  main, secondary and connecting roads) were not found.
- Field size: the 1986 source gives 100-200 ha, while the project's dressing rules make
  parcels of about 2-17 ha (trait T2).

## 0. Answers in short

- **Fields were big.** In 1985 a cooperative farmed about 4,270 ha on average and a state farm about 7,095 ha. Its
  fields were contiguous blocks of hundreds of hectares, and 100-200 ha fields were usual. The dressing's 2-17 ha
  parcels are right only for household plots and the village fringe. Proposed: TSZ parcels of 800-2,000 m by
  400-1,000 m (about 32-200 ha, mean about 100 ha), and furrow squares of 5-6 km. Small parcels stay within about
  1 km of a village (section 1.4).
- **The 1985 crop mix.** Sourced areas: wheat 1.36 Mha, maize 1.05 Mha, sunflower 0.34 Mha and barley 0.28 Mha, on

  industrial crops and 19.2% fodder crops. Proposed shares per class (wheat, maize, sunflower, other row crops,
  lucerne, ploughed, pasture) are in section 1.4.
- **Autumn 1985 was a dry autumn with a late harvest, which differs from the 2018 anchor in `fill_tech.md`.** There

- These ranges give 32-200 ha fields, a mean of about 98 ha (1,400 m by 700 m), and more than half of fields between

- **Why the heading square must grow.** In `FieldParcel` (`simcore/include/simcore/scenery/map_dressing.hpp`, lines
  254-269) every parcel is cut where its heading square ends, and the width is drawn once per square. With 3 km
  squares and 2 km fields, most parcels would be cut. At 5.5 km, about a quarter of the 1,400 m mean length is cut.
- **The village fringe keeps the small parcels** (new rule, design). Within `fringe_m` of a settlement's edge
  (proposed 800 m for a village, 1,200 m for a town; estimate), use the current 250-700 m by 80-250 m parcels. These
  are household plots (háztáji) and small strips, seen in P78 and P15. In code, `FieldParcel` would take the distance
  to the nearest settlement from the map's settlement list (centre and radius) and switch between the two parcel
  rules. That is cheap: one loop over about 11 settlements per cell, or a 250 m pre-pass grid.
- **The Kesmark district may be sand ridge country.** The map's origin, 46.9 N 19.7 E (`data/maps/kesmark_range.json`),
  falls on the Danube-Tisza interfluve near Kecskemét. That is sandy country with more tanyas, vineyards, orchards,

The anchor kinds, each resolved from what the map already lays out:

- `parcel_edge`: the nearest parcel edge to `near_km` whose parcel has `crop`, found by stepping `FieldParcel` across
  the furrows. The eye stands 2 m outside the edge, and `across_parcel` looks along the parcel's short axis.
- `settlement_street`: the settlement's centre and `heading_deg` (Kesmark has one; villages default to 0). The eye is
  `from_centre_m` along it, and the camera looks along it.
- `road_verge`: a point at fraction `at` along the road's polyline. The eye is `side_m` beyond the carriageway edge,
  looking along the road towards a settlement id.
- `farmyard`: a new site kind (see 2.4). If the map has none, the view is reported missing, not skipped silently.
- `farmstead`: the nearest generated tanya (`farmsteads`) to `near_km`. The eye is `from_m` off, in a field.
- `site`: a site id. `panorama` writes one image per `step_deg`, named `<id>_h000.png` to `<id>_h315.png`.
- `river_bank`: the river polyline's nearest point to `near_km`, with the eye `side_m` off its centreline.
  `dyke: true` needs the map to lay a dyke (see 2.4); until then the eye stands on the bare bank.
- `hill` and `settlement`: a point `from_m` from the centre on the bearing `bearing_from_deg`, looking at the centre.

### 2.3 Rendering and comparing (design)

1. **simcore, `simcore/include/simcore/scenery/acceptance_views.hpp`** (header-only, C++17, no exceptions; names
   that avoid the reserved macros such as `near` and `far`):
   - `LoadAcceptanceViews(json)` returns the views and skies, with errors as values.
   - `ResolveView(const TerrainMap&, const DressingRules&, seed, const AcceptanceView&)` returns
     `{ok, missing_reason, eye_enu, target_enu, fov_rad, heights}` per image.
   - A test, `simcore/tests/test_acceptance_views.cpp`, checks that every view on `kesmark_range.json` either
     resolves or is reported missing with a reason. For each resolved view it checks:
     - the eye is above the drawn ground (`MapData` levels);
     - the eye is not inside an obstacle footprint (`MapData::ObstacleTop`);
     - the target differs from the eye;
     - every photo id exists in the reference register (below).
2. **Unreal, `ASimMapActor::RunShots`:**
   - `-SimShotSet=acceptance` replaces the hard-coded list with the resolved views. Without the flag it keeps
     today's list.
   - Each shot sets `ShotCamera->GetCameraComponent()->SetFieldOfView(fov)` and the sky's clock and weather through
     the scenario (R7's `USimWorldSubsystem` sky). It writes `<folder>/<id>[_<sky>][_h<deg>].png`.
   - A missing view writes `<folder>/<id>.missing.txt` with the reason.
   - Proposed run: `-SimMapShots=C:/dev/SAM3D/out/acceptance -SimShotSet=acceptance -ResX=1920 -ResY=1080`.
     Whether `-RenderOffscreen` honours the resolution was not checked: Claude cannot run the editor.
   - The sky needs time to settle after a clock change, so allow 4-5 s rather than 2.5 s. This is an estimate.
3. **The scenario, `data/scenarios/acceptance_views.json`:** the Kesmark range with no targets, the October sky
   clock, and its `utc_offset_h` the same as the nation's theatre (dated 15 October 1985, roadmap R7).
4. **The reference register, `data/scenery/reference_photos.json`:** moves `references.md`'s table and this file's
   P71-P92 into data. Each entry has `id`, `page_url`, `image_url`, `licence`, `author`, `date`, `place`, `season`,
   `shows` and `views`. It holds **links only**: nothing is downloaded or redistributed, which keeps
   `tools/check_assets.py` and the asset register (`data/scenery/assets.json`) out of it. Image URLs:
   - Commons: `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/<file>?width=640`;
   - Fortepan: `https://fortepan.download/file/fortepan-eu/1600/fortepan_<id>.jpg`, the pattern the Fortepan site's
     own pages load. A Fortepan number on a Commons copy may no longer match Fortepan's own record (see P61), so the
     test should check each Fortepan id's year and place against the Fortepan search API.
5. **The compare page, `tools/acceptance_view.py out/acceptance`:** writes `out/acceptance/index.html`. Each view is
   one row:
   - the game's image or images;
   - the reference photos as thumbnails loaded from their pages' URLs at view time, each with its author and licence
     (the CC BY and CC BY-SA terms ask for attribution);
   - the must-see list as checkboxes for a person to tick.
   A `pytest` (`tools/test_acceptance_view.py`) checks that the register's licences are only CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA,
   or public domain.

### 2.4 What the Kesmark map lacks for the views

- **No TSZ farmyard (major).** `kesmark_range.json`'s 57 sites have no farm kind. V5 needs a `farmyard` site kind:
  long sheds, an open machine shelter, a dryer tower, a concrete-slab access road (350 m is cited in `references.md`
  T3) and tree rows. Building dimensions are in `fill_built.md` section 3.
- **No flood dyke.** The Rava has a valley 6 m deep (`terrain.valley`) but no dyke. V8's dyke-crest eye height of
  5-6 m assumes one.
- **Only gentle hills.** Polany hill is 55 m high, and the map's "northern uplands" are 150 km away and 300 m high.
  V9 is therefore a stand-in for the Mátra and Cserhát views. The country map (`maps/pannonia.json`) would serve
  better, but it is not drawn at the same detail.

## 7. Method

- **Newspapers.** Délmagyarország 1985 issues were fetched as PDFs from the EPrints archive into a scratch folder,
  read with `pdftotext`, then deleted. Records and PDFs follow the pattern in 1.3.
- **Statistics.** IndexMundi's USDA PSD pages and MEK's table were read as web pages.
- **Commons.** The MediaWiki API was searched by category and text (`list=search` in namespaces 6 and 14,
  `categorymembers`, `imageinfo` with `extmetadata`) with a 1.5 s pause between calls. Images were viewed through
  `Special:FilePath/<file>?width=800` in the browser pane.
- **Fortepan.** The Elasticsearch proxy in section 5 was queried by tag and text with a year range and by `mid`.
  Images were viewed through `https://fortepan.download/file/fortepan-eu/1600/fortepan_<mid>.jpg`.
- **Not reachable this session:** Hungaricana (403), real-j.mtak.hu, regi.tankonyvtar.hu, AgEcon Search,
  agroinform and ResearchGate (403), and a CIA reading-room PDF that redirected.
- Only links are recorded. No image or asset file is kept.
