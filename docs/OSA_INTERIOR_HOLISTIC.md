# The Osa's interior, seen whole: the holistic layer

Written 8 November 2026 (project owner's request: "an additional holistic layer, where the reference image is observed at
face value, and every single component is classified and identified ... rough schematics ... fuse sources"). It sits
in front of the measurement pipeline (`docs/OSA_INTERIOR_PIPELINE.md`) and complements it: the pipeline measures one
face to the millimetre; this layer says **what is in the compartment, where each thing is relative to everything else,
and which source says so**, so that nothing is modelled in the wrong place or left out, and so the pipeline knows which
faces to trace next.

**Its second half, the look-alike check** (9 Nov 2026, project owner: "an extra step in the pipeline that cross-references
imagery with the 3d output ... include minute visual details, shapes, geometry and every small detail present in the
reference"): every photo is posed on the cabin, the built cabin is rendered from that photo's camera, and the two are
compared component by component, edge by edge and detail by detail (sections 11-14). The register says what is there;
the look-alike check says whether the model looks like it, and lists everything small the model still lacks. The
measurement pipeline is unchanged: the two layers meet only through the files named in section 14.

## 1. The products

| Product | File | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Observations** | `tools/traces/osa_interior/observations/<photo>.json` | One file per photograph: the photo looked at as it is, every visible component listed and classified, with where it is in the image, what it is mounted on and next to, and how sure the observer is. No measurement, no guessing past the frame. |
| **Component register** | `data/cabins/osa_ak_components.json` | One entry per physical component of the 9A33BM2's compartment (hull, racks, blocks, boxes, seats, lamps, fans, ducts, cables, brackets, shelves, plates...), with a canonical id, its class, its zone, its parent, its box in the body frame with an uncertainty, every source that places it, conflicts between sources, and how far the 3-D cabin has got with it. |
| **Schematics** | `out/recon/holistic/*.svg`, `index.html` (generated by `tools/recon/holistic.py draw`) | The register drawn: the plan from above, the left and right walls, the front (cab) and the rear bulkhead, the roof from below; each component a box coloured by class, outlined by confidence, with Fig. 3 underneath where it covers the view. Rough by design: a gauge of where things sit, for reference. |
| **Views** | `tools/traces/osa_interior/views/<photo>.json` | One file per posed photo: how its camera is found (from a face the measurement pipeline solved, or from anchors: named points of the cabin clicked in the photo). Section 11. |
| **Detail inventories** | `tools/traces/osa_interior/details/<photo>.json` | One file per photo: every small thing the photo shows below the register's components (fasteners, seams, edges, ribs, openings, hinges, latches, handles, labels, clips, grommets, gaskets, patterns, profiles, surfaces, small parts), each on its parent component, with its place in the image, relief, how it is to be built and whether the model has it. Section 12. |
| **Look-alike results** | `out/recon/holistic/views/<photo>/` (generated): `sheet.jpg`, `lacking.jpg`, `compare.json`, `details_3d.json`; the report `docs/research/OSA_INTERIOR_LOOKALIKE_2026-11.md` | The photo beside the model rendered from its camera; the model's edges over the photo with the photo's edges the model lacks; each observed component against the model's; each detail cast into the cabin. Section 11. |

`tools/recon/holistic.py` checks the files (`check`), merges the observations into the register's evidence (`fuse`),
draws the schematics (`draw`) and writes the coverage report `docs/research/OSA_INTERIOR_COMPONENTS_2026-11.md`
(`report`): every component, its sources, its status in the cabin, and what is still missing.
`tools/recon/holistic_view.py` runs the look-alike check (`check`, `pose`, `render`, `compare`, `cast`, `report`, `all`),
with `tools/blender/holistic_scene.py` assembling the cabin in Blender as Unreal places it.

## 2. Classes

Every component gets exactly one class. The classes cover everything a photo of the compartment can show:

| Group | Classes |
|---|---|
| Structure | `hull_wall`, `floor`, `roof`, `bulkhead`, `hatch`, `window`, `door`, `pylon` (the turret's base through the compartment), `sponson` (wheel arch, tunnel or box in the floor or wall), `frame` (rack post, rail, stiffener), `lining` (insulation, padding) |
| Equipment | `rack` (a cabinet of blocks), `block` (one unit in a rack, with its face), `console` (a desk-type panel), `box` (a free-standing or wall box: radio power unit, spares box, junction box), `radio`, `intercom`, `phone`, `instrument` (a gauge, compass or sight standing on its own), `fan`, `heater`, `filter` (FVU), `periscope`, `motor` (wiper motor and similar), `power` (connector, battery, converter) |
| Crew | `seat`, `bench`, `belt`, `cushion`, `footrest`, `curtain`, `handle` (a grab handle), `step` |
| Fittings | `bracket`, `shelf`, `mount` (shock mount), `clamp`, `hook`, `strap`, `cover` (a lid, a cap, a floor cover), `rail` (curtain rail, guard rail), `plate` (a warning plate, decal, table on a wall) |
| Runs | `cable` (one cable), `loom` (a bundle), `conduit`, `hose`, `duct`, `pipe` |
| Light and controls outside the faces | `lamp`, `switch`, `connector`, `fuse_panel`, `control` (pedal, lever, wheel) |
| Loose | `kit` (headset, first-aid box, fire extinguisher, tool, document holder), `unknown` |

A block's face (its knobs, lamps, plates) is the trace pipeline's job: the observation names the block and stops.

## 3. Ids

Dotted, lower case, ASCII: `<group>.<name>[.<part>]`, e.g. `rack.sp2m`, `block.sp2m.or31m`, `radio.r123m_left`,
`fan.roof_front_right`, `cable.loom_sp2m_top`. The register's seed holds 163 ids from Fig. 3, the cabin data and
the book's notes. An observer uses a register id when the component is one of them, and proposes a new one with the
prefix `new.` otherwise (`new.bracket.under_radio_right`); the fuse step turns accepted proposals into register entries.
A component seen in several photos keeps one id: that is how the photos are tied together.

## 4. An observation file

```json
{
  "photo": "g092-a",
  "vehicle": "9A33BM3-1 ex-NVA (Lešany?)",
  "camera": {"stands": "in the aisle at the rear, about 1.4 m up", "looks": "forward-right along the right wall",
             "covers": ["rack.sp2m", "rack.sp1m", "cab"], "lens": "wide"},
  "components": [
    {"id": "rack.sp2m", "class": "rack", "seen_as": "grey rack of 18 blocks", "bbox": [0.02, 0.0, 0.66, 0.93],
     "zone": "compartment.right_wall", "on": "hull.wall_right",
     "relations": [["forward_of", "rack.sp3m"], ["aft_of", "rack.sp1m"]],
     "size_cue": "reaches from the floor's kick strip to the roof rail", "material": "grey enamel",
     "confidence": "high", "notes": ""}
  ],
  "not_in_register": ["a bundle of three cables along the roof rail above SP-2M"],
  "doubts": ["the box under SP-1M may be ZCzZ1A nr 2 or a battery box"]
}
```

- `bbox`: the component's box in the image, fractions of width and height (x0, y0, x1, y1), as much of it as shows.
- `zone`: `cab`, `cab.dash`, `cab.left_wall`, `cab.right_wall`, `cab.roof`, `compartment.left_wall`,
  `compartment.right_wall`, `compartment.aisle`, `compartment.floor`, `compartment.roof`, `compartment.rear`,
  `rear_bulkhead`, `engine` (beyond the bulkhead), `outside`.
- `relations`: `on` (mounted on), `in` (inside), `above`, `below`, `forward_of`, `aft_of`, `left_of`, `right_of`
  (the vehicle's left and right, not the image's), `beside`, `behind` (further from the aisle), `in_front_of`,
  `connects` (a run's two ends), `same_as` (two ids that are one thing).
- `size_cue`: what the photo shows of its size, against something known (a block 0.6 m wide, an R-123M 406 mm, a hand,
  a seat), not a number invented.
- `confidence`: `high` (plainly seen and named), `medium` (seen, identity inferred), `low` (glimpsed or guessed).

## 5. A register entry

```json
{"id": "radio.r123m_left", "class": "radio", "name": "R-123M (left)", "names": ["R-123M"],
 "zone": "cab.left_wall", "parent": null,
 "box_m": {"centre": [-0.627, 3.28, 1.24], "size": [0.166, 0.481, 0.244], "sigma_m": 0.05, "from": "cabin:radio_left"},
 "plan": {"sheet": "p028", "px": [793, 1010, 912, 1043]},
 "cabin": ["radio_left", "radio_left_panel"], "status": "traced",
 "sources": ["mon_2112_80 Fig. 3", "wwp_gecko g090-c", "wwp_gecko g091-b"],
 "conflicts": ["Fig. 3 puts it at y 3.03, the cabin at 3.28 (by SŁ-1M)"], "notes": ""}
```

- `box_m`: axis-aligned box in the body frame (x right, y forward, z up, metres): centre and size, with one sigma. Its
  `from` says which source set it.
- `plan` / `elev`: the component on Fig. 3, as pixel boxes on `fig_p028` / `fig_p029` (`elev` gives the section `A-A`
  or `B-B`). The tool turns them into body coordinates (§6).
- `status`: `missing` (not in the cabin), `placed` (a plain stand-in at its place), `modelled` (a mesh built by rule
  or estimate), `traced` (built from a photo trace), `face_traced` (and its face traced to ±2 mm).

## 6. Sources and how they are fused

Each position comes from the best source that gives it, in this order, and the others are kept as checks:

1. **A trace** (`tools/blender/<name>_trace.py`, `tools/traces/`): measured, ±1-10 mm.
2. **The cabin data** where it was measured (Fig. 3 plan, SAM Simulator's straight-on photos): ±10-30 mm.
3. **Fig. 3** (`mon_2112_80`, the Polish manual's plan with the A-A and B-B wall sections). Calibrated on
   8 Nov 2026 against the cabin data: 4.86 mm a pixel along and across on both sheets; forward is to the left;
   on `p028` the centreline is at py 909 and SŁ-2M's front end at px 960 (y 2.505); on `p029` the centreline is at
   py 917 and SŁ-2M's front end at px 95. The sections are squashed in height (5.74 mm a pixel, the racks' tops at
   z 2.09): good for what is above what, not for heights. ±30-50 mm in plan.
4. **The text** (manuals, the book's captions): which block is where, what a box is for.
5. **The photos at face value** (the observations): relations and size cues. They place what Fig. 3 leaves out (cables,
   brackets, lamps, plates, the turret pylon) to about ±50-150 mm, and they settle Fig. 3's conflicts when several
   photos agree.

When two sources disagree by more than their sigmas the register keeps both under `conflicts` and says which was used;
a photo trace or a camera solve settles it later (roadmap I1). The book's photos show a 9A33BM3-1: decision D1 holds
(shape from the BM3-1 where the AK shares the block).

## 7. How it feeds the pipeline and the model

- **The trace queue**: every component whose status is below `traced` and that a photo shows square-on is a face for
  the pipeline (`faces`), listed in the report with its best photo.
- **Placement**: the register's boxes go into `data/cabins/osa_ak.json` as shell parts or fixtures only when their
  sigma is below the roadmap's target for that class; the rest wait for a trace or a solve.
- **Cross-checks**: the trace of one face is checked against the register's neighbours (SP-2M2's bottom at z 0.85 must
  sit on the kick strip that g092-a shows), and the register's relations are checked against the built cabin
  (`holistic.py check --cabin`: a component the photos put above another must be above it in the data).
- **Nothing invented**: a component no photo, drawing or text places stays `missing` with its reason; it is not
  modelled until a source places it.
- **The look-alike check closes the loop** (sections 11-14): what the register places and the builders build is rendered
  from every reference photo's camera and compared with it; what does not look like the photo goes back into the
  register's tables, the observations, the detail inventories and the builders, and is rendered again.

## 8. How the register is kept

The register is **generated**: `holistic.py seed --force` writes its base from the tables in `tools/recon/holistic.py`
(the cabin data's panels, shell parts and blocks; Fig. 3's items with their pixel boxes; the named items; the known
conflicts), and `holistic.py fuse` adds the photos' evidence and accepted proposals on top. So a correction goes into
`holistic.py`'s tables or into an observation file, never into the JSON by hand; the two commands rebuild it. Fuse's
rules: a proposal (`new.*`) becomes an entry when one photo of the 1980s fit shows it with medium confidence or better;
photos of a later vehicle (g101, the Czech 2000s fit) and items outside the compartment give evidence for existing ids
only; an unmeasured component's place is the weighted mean of what its relations say (`estimate`, sigma 0.15 m with four
or more relations, 0.25 with fewer, 0.4 from its zone alone).

## 11. The look-alike check: the photo against the model (H6, H7)

Added 9 Nov 2026. The first pass placed the major assets well, but the model did not look like the photos: the racks'
frames, the walls' shape and the things round the instruments were wrong or missing, and nothing in the layer said
so. This step makes the photo and the model meet, picture to picture.

**A view** (`tools/traces/osa_interior/views/<photo>.json`) says how the photo's camera is found, best first:

| `camera_from` | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| `face` | The camera the measurement pipeline solved on a traced face (its `R`, `t` in the face's frame; for example g092-a on SP-2M2's flattened front, rms 3.3 px), moved to the body frame by the face's panel in the cabin data | Everything else in the photo is checked against that one measured face, not fitted to it |
| `anchors` | Six or more named points of the cabin clicked in the photo: `panel:<id>@tl`, `shell:<id>@+-0`, `reg:<id>@+0+` or `body:x,y,z`. The focal length is searched over 0.35-6 frame widths, with PnP and Levenberg-Marquardt for each. The principal point is held at the centre (cropped prints) | Photos with no traced face. Each anchor is also left out in turn: an anchor that the others put far from its click is a model point in the wrong place, which is itself a finding |

When the measurement pipeline's `room` or `ba` stage solves a photo, its camera replaces the view's own. Until then the
view's camera is the visual layer's own, and every result says so.

A view also carries:
- **The photographer's room** (`eye_box`; by default the compartment's aisle: x -0.55 to 0.45, y -0.95 to 4.3, z 0.8 to
  2.2). Anchors on one plane, such as a console's face, leave the focal length and the distance trading off at almost
  the same residual. On g094-a the rms moved only from 28.7 to 31 px between a 50-degree and a 95-degree field, and
  the free solve put the eye inside the SP-2M2 rack. The solve takes the best focal length whose eye is inside the
  box.
- **Checks** (`"use": false` with a `why`): model points thought to be off, such as the 0096-9M's turned face. They
  are reported against the camera the other anchors give, but not fitted to.
- **The lens's barrel** (`k1` with its source), taken from the measurement pipeline's `calib` stage (plumb lines on
  the clean scan; read, never rerun). The clicks are undistorted before the solve, and the detail rays and the
  comparison use the same term.
- **The vehicle's state** (`state.hide` with a `why`): cabin parts that are not as built in the vehicle photographed.
  In every photo of the BM3-1 the operators' seat backs are folded down (g095-d, g096-a). The render leaves those
  parts out, so the photo's floor is not counted against seats the cabin builds up.
- **`pending`** with a `why`, when no camera can honestly be fitted. That happens when the cabin has too little of
  what the photo shows, or when what it has disagrees with itself as the photo sees it (the anchors fit no camera
  better than several per cent of the width). The anchors clicked so far are kept, and the pose runs when the zone is
  built or the pipeline solves the photo. A pending photo's detail inventory still stands; its details are cast once
  it has a camera.

**The render** (`holistic_view.py render`, Blender in the background, `tools/blender/holistic_scene.py`):
- The cabin as Unreal places it:
  - every shell part's mesh at its centre and yaw;
  - the panels' plates and baked faces;
  - the blocks and the fixtures.
- Seen from the photo's camera, at the photo's aspect.
- Two passes:
  - `model`: Workbench, studio light, the meshes' colours and the baked faces;
  - `id`: every cabin part one flat colour, no anti-aliasing and no dither, with a map back to the part.
- In the same run, every detail's pixel is cast as a ray against the built cabin (section 12).

**The comparison** (`holistic_view.py compare`) works at a width of 1600 px, with the photo undistorted by its camera's
k1. It has four parts:

1. **Edges.**
   - Canny edges on the photo (bilateral-filtered) and on the model render, plus the id pass's part boundaries.
   - A model edge is *supported* when a photo edge lies within 0.5 % of the width (8 px). A photo edge is *explained*
     when a model edge does.
   - The photo's unexplained edges are **what the model lacks**: a cornice, a strut, a printed table, a seam.
     `lacking.jpg` draws them in magenta over the photo, with the model's edges in cyan.
   - Their density in 8 × 6 tiles ranks where to look first, with the observed components over each tile.
2. **Components.** Each component that the photo's observation names is found in the model in one of three ways: by
   its cabin parts in the id pass, by a block's rectangle on its panel, or by its register box projected (for a
   component the cabin lacks). The verdicts:
   - `agrees`: box overlap IoU ≥ 0.5; `off`: 0.2-0.5; `wrong place`: below 0.2.
   - `the model has it elsewhere`: the cabin has it, but not in this view. `hidden in the model`: in view, but behind
     something.
   - `only a box in the register` or `register box off`: not built; its estimated place agrees, or doesn't.
   - `not in the model`: it has no place at all.
   - `structure: judge by the edges`: for the hull, which is one mesh.

   The observers drew their boxes by eye, so IoU is a screen, not a measurement.
3. **The model's parts nobody named.** These are parts covering more than 0.4 % of the frame that no observation names.
   Each one is hidden in the photo, absent from the vehicle photographed, or in the wrong place in the model; each is
   looked at.
4. **Details.** The photo's detail inventory, drawn on it by status: absent red, blocked orange, modelled blue, matched
   green.

**Outputs:**
- `sheet.jpg` puts the four side by side: the photo with its details, the model from the camera, the edges, and the
  components.
- `compare.json` holds the numbers.
- `holistic_view.py report` gathers every photo's results into `docs/research/OSA_INTERIOR_LOOKALIKE_2026-11.md`.

**A photo passes** when, over the zones its roadmap step builds:

| Measure | Pass |
|---|---|
| The photo's structural edges explained (the printed text of plates excluded: it is a decal, section 12) | ≥ 90 % within 0.5 % of the width |
| The model's edges supported | ≥ 85 % (an unsupported model edge is a part the photo does not have, or one in the wrong place) |
| Components | every one `agrees`, or carries its reason in the register (`NOT_BUILT`, a conflict, a later fit) |
| Parts nobody named | each explained (hidden, absent from the BM3-1 photographed, or moved) |
| Details | every `high` and `medium` detail `modelled` or `matched`, or `not_built` with its reason; `matched` once a re-render shows it within 0.5 % of the width of where the photo has it |

**The loop**, per zone:
1. Observe (H1).
2. Register (H2-H3).
3. Build (the I steps).
4. Pose and render (H6).
5. Compare (H7).
6. Put what is wrong into the register's tables, the observations, the inventories and the builders.
7. Render again.

A zone is done when every photo of it passes.

## 12. Minute details (H8, H9)

The register stops at components, but a model looks right only when the small things are there too. A **detail
inventory** (`tools/traces/osa_interior/details/<photo>.json`) lists them.

**How a photo is read for details:**
- Use the raw scan, not the descreened one: descreening softens the small things.
- Blur it lightly to quiet the halftone.
- Cut it into nine tiles at full size, with a grid every 0.02 of the image.
- Read tile by tile against the model rendered from the same camera, asking of every small thing: "does the model have
  this?"
- List everything. A repeated thing is listed once, with its count (`pattern`), at one example's place.

**A detail:**

```json
{"id": "d05", "on": "hull.wall_right", "kind": "fastener",
 "what": "a strip along the top of the wall aft of SP-2M2, under the cornice, with a row of six hex bolt heads",
 "line": [[0.86, 0.225], [0.99, 0.245]], "pattern": {"count": 6}, "relief": "raised", "size_cue": "heads about 12 mm",
 "build": "mesh", "confidence": "medium", "status": "absent"}
```

- `on`: the register component it belongs to (an id or a `new.` proposal), so that the detail is built with its parent.
- Its place: one of `point`, `points`, `line` (a polyline: a seam, a cable, an edge, a profile) or `bbox`, in image
  fractions.
- `relief`: `raised`, `recessed`, `flush` or `through`.
- `size_cue`: a size against something known, never an invented number.
- `status`:
  - `absent`: the model lacks it;
  - `blocked`: a stand-in shape (a square plate where the photo's is rounded);
  - `modelled`;
  - `matched`: seen in place in a re-render;
  - `not_built`: a later fit's, with `why`.

**The kinds** (`DETAIL_KINDS` in `holistic_view.py`), each with its default way of being built:

| Kind | What | Built as |
|---|---|---|
| `fastener` | screw, bolt, nut, rivet or stud head | mesh |
| `seam` | joint between plates, weld bead, gap | normal map |
| `edge` | chamfer, fillet, rolled lip, flange, step | mesh |
| `rib` | stiffener, swaged bead, frame member, channel post | mesh |
| `opening` | hole, vent, louvre, slot, grille, cut-out; an open front | mesh |
| `hinge`, `latch`, `handle` | | mesh |
| `label` | plate, stencil, sticker, printed table | decal |
| `clip`, `grommet`, `gasket` | cable clip, tie, chain; gland, cable entry; rubber seal | mesh |
| `pattern` | perforation, tread plate, mesh, quilting, knurling, a curtain's folds | normal map (mesh where it makes the silhouette) |
| `profile` | the outline of a structural surface: a wall break, a cornice, a sponson's edge, a slope | mesh |
| `surface` | paint, two-tone, wear, dents, rust, stains, padding | texture |
| `part` | a small component the register does not list | mesh |

**The build rule** (`build_rule`):
- Geometry for anything that makes the silhouette, or that stands 2 mm or more proud of (or sunk into) its surface at
  arm's length from a crew eye point.
- A normal map, baked from geometry, for relief of 0.3-2 mm.
- A decal or texture for what is flat.
- More than 1.5 m from every seat's eye point, geometry under 5 mm drops to the normal map.

**Cast into the cabin** (`holistic_view.py cast`, H9). Each detail's pixel becomes a ray from the photo's camera. At
render time Blender casts it against the built cabin and records the surface it meets, the part and its normal. When
the model lacks the detail's parent, the ray is also met with the parent's register box. `details_3d.json` then says,
for each detail, one of:
- *on its parent in the model*: its place is as good as the model's accuracy, ready for the builder;
- *on its parent's register box*: as good as the box's sigma;
- *a ray only, in front of* the surface named: the thing stands between the camera and that surface, and a second
  photo's ray must cross it (H4).

So every detail reaches the builders as a 3-D place or a ray, never as a description alone.

**How the details get into the model.** The I steps build them; this is the method.

1. **A detail kit** (`tools/blender/detail_kit.py`, roadmap I10): parametric parts for every kind built as a mesh.
   - The parts:
     - fasteners: slotted, cross-head and hex screw heads; dome and countersunk rivets; hex bolts and studs;
     - weld beads (a swept bumpy tube);
     - rolled lips and flanges (a bevel profile);
     - channel, angle and tube sections (extruded);
     - knuckle and piano hinges, toggle latches, U-handles on feet;
     - P-clips, saddles, ties and ball chains; grommets and glands;
     - louvres and slots (cut), perforated plate, quilted padding, cloth folds.
   - Each part is sized from its detail's `size_cue` and from sizes the traces have measured (from the SP-2M2 trace:
     screws 6.2 mm, lamps 10.8, jacks 20.1, locks 27).
   - It is placed from `details_3d.json` on its parent's surface, along its normal.
   - It is instanced in Unreal (one mesh, many transforms), so a rack's three hundred screws cost one draw.
2. **Profiles become sections.**
   - A structural `profile` (the cornice, the roof's step, the wheel arches' housings, the sloped upper walls) seen in
     two or more photos gives the hull's inner cross-section at that station.
   - The shell is rebuilt from these sections (lofted) instead of from boxes.
   - Until a second photo's ray crosses it, a profile is drawn on its wall at the observed height and marked so.
3. **Normal maps and trim sheets.** Seams, small rivets seen from a distance, knurling, embossed letters and tread are
   modelled once in the kit at full detail. They are baked to a shared trim sheet that the shell's and the racks' UVs
   use.
4. **Decals from the photos.**
   - Plates, printed tables and stencils are flattened from the photo that shows them best (`photo_trace.py rectify`,
     as a face), then vectorised (Potrace).
   - Text is re-set in a matching typeface only where it has been read and confirmed by two readers (the pipeline's
     plate rule).
   - Text that has not been read stays a blank plate, never an invented one.
5. **Surface.**
   - The paint of each part comes from the photos, with the white labels as the neutral (I6).
   - Wear and grime masks are painted where the photos show them: edges, handles, the floor's walkway.

## 14. How the two layers meet

The measurement pipeline (`docs/OSA_INTERIOR_PIPELINE.md`) is unchanged. The look-alike check reads what the pipeline
makes and gives it work, but never writes its files:

| From the pipeline | To the look-alike check |
|---|---|
| A face's solved camera (`faces`, the SP-2M2 trace's g092-a camera) | a view's `face` camera |
| `room` / `ba` cameras, once built | they replace the views' own `anchors` cameras |
| The traced faces' meshes (SM_SP2M2Face) | rendered like every other part; their details count as `modelled` |

| From the look-alike check | To the pipeline |
|---|---|
| The photo edges the model lacks, by tile, with the components over them | the trace queue: a face whose zone stays unexplained is traced next |
| Details that land on a traced face (`details_3d.json`, *on its parent in the model*) | candidates for that face's `detect` and `verify` (chains, seals, legends) |
| Anchors whose left-out residual is large | a model point in the wrong place, for `ba` to look at first |
| Profiles cast into the cabin | the shell's sections for I1, to be measured by `room` / `ba` |

The pipeline proves faces in millimetres; the look-alike check proves the whole picture, including everything no face
trace covers. Both must pass before a zone is done (roadmap section 2.3).

## 32. The comparison that draws conclusions (9 Nov 2026)

**Why.** Sections 25-31 produced pictures and clouds, and a person had to look at them and say what was wrong. Looking at g096-a's depth maps side by side, the owner could see the 0096-9M2 at the wrong angle, the raised floor under it missing, the side slope and roofline different; nothing in the pipeline said so. This stage says so: in words, in metres, per part, and with a score that shows whether an edit helped.

**The stage** (`tools/recon/holistic_diff.py`, run under `E:\SAM3D-tools\venv-depth`; `tools/recon/holistic_refine.py` before it. The measuring pipeline is unchanged):

| Step | Command | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Adjust the cameras | `holistic_refine.py fov`, `ba` | A bundle adjustment over the solve's own tracks (Levenberg-Marquardt with the points eliminated, numpy only) with what COLMAP could not know: SP-2M2's traced face (the frame), SL-2M's measured plane, the across-aisle narrowing. Writes `sfm/refined/`, which `holistic_view.py` and `holistic_depth.py` then use |
| The model as triangles | `holistic_diff.py scene` | The built cabin out of Blender with each triangle's part, so a million rays take a quarter of a second in Embree |
| Register each view | `views` | The photo's depth map is put onto the model by a trimmed projective ICP on **the parts traced in millimetres only** (`ANCHORS`). A wrong part cannot pull the camera, so it stays an error. The lens comes from a grid search, because the map's scale, the eye's distance and the focal length cannot be told apart otherwise. A registration may only correct the solve: past 0.30 m or 6 degrees from the solve's camera (`within_limits`) the solve's camera stands. A view that does not land (under `MIN_ON_MODEL` of its traced pixels on the model; with the solve's camera, under `MIN_AGREE_UNANCHORED` agreeing) still gets its sheet, but its findings are not used |
| Compare per pixel | `views` | Model depth and normals against the photo's, through one camera, at 800 px. A pixel agrees within the larger of 4 cm and 2.5 % of its range, and within 30 degrees of direction |
| Findings | `views`, `findings` | Disagreeing pixels are grouped by connected region and by part. Each region gets a plane fit on both sides, and a kind: **turned** (both planar, at different angles), **raise** (hull floor higher in the photos), **missing** (the photo has something in front of the model), **excess** (the model stands in front of the photo), **moved** (a shift along the normal, with its vector), **shape**. Regions of the same kind and part sharing grid cells across views are one finding, ranked by area and support |
| Part poses | `poses` | Each movable part fitted to the photos' surfaces by trimmed point-to-plane ICP, yaw and translation, bounded to a correction. Only the ones that clearly improve are reported |
| Surfaces | `surfaces` | Floor, roof, both walls and the bulkhead as height maps from the **same pixels** on both sides, photo, model and difference, with metre ticks |
| Score | `score`, `baseline` | Share of pixels agreeing and median miss per view, against a kept baseline. This is the loop's gate: an edit that does not raise it did not help |
| Measure | `measure <photo> --poly ...` | A region of a registered photo as a plane in metres (centre, normal, yaw, tilt, extent), beside the model through the same pixels. How a finding becomes a number to put in the data |
| Side by side | `render <photos>` | The model rendered in Blender through each registered camera, beside the photo (`renders/<photo>.jpg`), with what faces down lifted so a roof's shape shows. For the eye: what the numbers miss, such as a lid standing open |

Sheets: `out/recon/holistic/diff/sheets/<photo>.jpg` (photo, its depth, the model's depth, the difference, then the numbered findings on the photo with their conclusions). Written report: `docs/research/OSA_INTERIOR_DIFF_2026-11.md`. What a photo shows that is not the vehicle as built (bags, cloth) goes in the view's trace as `"loose"` polygons, each with its reason, and is left out.

**The loop.** `views` and `findings`; read the sheets, then `measure` what they name; put the number in the data or the trace with its source; rebuild; `scene`, `views`, `findings`, `surfaces`, `score`. Keep the change if the score rises or the finding goes away.

**How far to trust it.** A depth network is good at shape and poor at absolute distance, so far surfaces are compressed (in g096-a the bulkhead reads y -0.67 where it is -1.035). What is near and what is planar is reliable; what is far and what is only a percent of the frame is not. A plane's angle carries the view's own residual camera error, read off a surface of known direction in the same view (g096-a: the bulkhead reads 6 degrees of yaw and 8 of tilt where both are 0). Corrections are therefore cross-checked (a part's plane against its pose solve; a surface in a second view or in the fused map) before they go in, and the numbers that go in cite the photo and the method.

**Limits.** Thirteen of the 24 compartment photos do not pass (section 33), mostly close-ups showing no traced part or views from the left of the aisle. The cab group is not to scale (section 30) and is not compared. The grid search chooses among a few lenses; the across-aisle narrowing is imposed from two independent measurements, not found. The tracks fit either width (1.01 px against 0.99 px reprojection), so they cannot confirm it.

**What the first attempts taught.** Focal-length priors alone moved nothing: 22 000 observations outweigh 47 priors. scipy's sparse least squares stalled and reported success; the bundle adjuster here converges in seconds. A plane prior alone sheared the cabin (the walkway moved 0.3 m), so y and z are held and the narrowing is uniform. Letting ICP free the scale collapsed maps onto one wall, so it is bounded. Registering on the whole model let a wrong cabinet turn the camera 15 degrees, which is why only traced parts place a camera.

## 34. Depth fused with the traces and the perspective (10 Nov 2026)

**Why.** Section 33's limit was the depth network: right about shape, wrong about distance, so the far wall read 10 to 20 cm off and agreement stayed low even where a render lined up. The traces and the solve are the opposite: exact, but only at places. `tools/recon/holistic_metric.py` puts them together, inside `holistic_diff.py views`.

**How.** Each map is multiplied by a correction field, smooth except across the map's own depth steps, pinned to the **perspective** (the solved camera and every triangulated point this photo saw) and to the **traces** (the parts traced in millimetres, cast through the same camera). Between the pins the shape stays the network's and the distances become the pins'. A pin that disagrees with its neighbours (a cable in front of a rack) is turned down.

**The test is withheld pins.** A quarter of each image's cells keep their pins out of the fit, and the miss on them is measured. Over the 24 compartment views the median miss went from 7.2 cm (the map with a low-order fit) to 2.6 cm at the chosen settings (`SMOOTH` 300, `PRIOR` 0.03, the best of nine). Three things follow:

- Pinned pixels agree with the model by construction, so they are no evidence: the score now counts the other pixels (`agree`; `agree_all` keeps the old figure). **Scores from section 33 and earlier are not comparable with these.**
- A view is used if its fused map meets its withheld pins within the agreement band's own rule, the larger of 5 cm and 2.5 % of the range, in place of the 'on the model' gate that a wrong camera could pass.
- Each view keeps whichever camera, the solve's or the registered one, meets its withheld pins better; a tie stays with the solve. 21 of the 24 keep the solve's.

**Limits.** A part with no pin takes its neighbours' correction whole: its shape is the network's, its distance borrowed. g096-a, the owner's example view, is still not used (10 cm on its withheld pins, though better than the 20 cm before); its sheet is still written. One random hold-out per view is a noisy measure for a single view; the median over views is the number to trust. The depth maps were regenerated with the adjusted cameras' lenses (a fresh map of g096-a differed by 2.2 % from the old).

**What it found first.** The walkway's forward step is only right of the channel: forward of y 1.45 the floor reads 0.79-0.80 left of it and 0.675 right of it (`floor.spine.forward_step.x_from_m`). Multi-view findings not yet acted on: the left ledge above the racks at about x -1.04, y 1.7 (26 degrees, 5 views); the post at about x 0.70, y 0.31 (22 degrees, 5 views).

**Score on this measure.** 21 of 24 views used, mean agreement 28.5 %, median miss 0.066 m, after the step (28.1 % before it, on the same views).

**Install.** `setup_tools.ps1`'s depth step was run from nothing on 10 Nov 2026 (`venv-depth` removed, MoGe cloned afresh): it installs, MoGe runs on the GPU, Embree casts, and `holistic_depth.py mono` remade all 76 maps.
