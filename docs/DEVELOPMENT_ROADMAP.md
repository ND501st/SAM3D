## Principles

0. **Crew and teamwork (the project owner's design pillars, 16 Oct 2026; `docs/crew/README.md`).** Teamwork with NPC
   crewmates is paramount. The game has two halves: *in the crew*, the player is one member of the team, learning the
   drill and how to operate each seat; *in command*, the player gives the orders, first for the single Osa, then for the
   battery. Every NPC works with every other; every action and command is mapped and interconnected (the generated action
   map), with behaviour paths, callouts and commands for every environment and outcome. NPC skill levels come from player
   progression and quests and affect reaction, speed and accuracy everywhere. An ideal progression of events, the drill
   master's perfect performance, is the reference for the training overlay, like a chess engine that always knows the
   best move. One crewmate's action depends on others' knowledge and actions (someone must know where the target is,
   someone must have driven within reach), and that interdependence is mapped.
1. **simcore is the game, Unreal is the presentation.** Build and test behaviour in simcore first; Unreal only displays it.
2. **Procedures and consoles are the heart of the game.** Operator procedures, interlocks, crew, battery and scenarios
   come before any deeper physics.
3. **Accuracy before features.** When research shows the model is wrong, fix the data model before building UI on it.
4. **Vertical slice first.** One system (Osa-AK), one battery position, one complete mission, before a second system.
5. **Small PRs on branches**, one topic each. Never commit to `main` directly.
6. **Definition of done:** builds with warnings-as-errors, tests added, `tools/verify.ps1` passes, docs updated if
   behaviour or data format changed. Unreal changes: built from the command line and checked with
   `tools/ue_cabin_check.py` where it applies; the user plays it.
7. **Public and declassified sources only, best tier first** (`docs/SOURCES.md`). Every data value has `source` and
   `confidence`; disagreements between sources are recorded, not hidden. SAM Simulator's art, sounds and `sam.exe`
   never enter git.

## Track R: the review of 2 Nov 2026 (priority)

The project owner, 2 Nov 2026, on reviewing the dynamic campaign test (`docs/DYNAMIC_CAMPAIGN_TEST.md`): in priority
order, first a preliminary damage model built on probabilities. It matters most for air-bursting AAA: flak bursts
close to an aircraft (5-50 m) very often do no damage. Second, the AAA is far too accurate at long range. Its spread
and its fuzing are the same as at close range, so with damage modelled the guns would be unrealistic and unbalanced.
Third, ground vehicles and even aircraft are drawn beneath the map. Fourth, the map needs more realism: terrain and more
features. Free Fab, Epic and Unreal assets, and 3D models found online, may be used as placeholders. Fifth, aircraft do
not pitch, yaw or roll realistically. Sixth, an engaged aircraft whose warning receiver sounds, or whose pilot sees
"smoke in the air", should manoeuvre to defend itself and sometimes jettison its tanks and its load. Seventh, a time of
day setting in the missions menu.

Like the nation on the map and track G, this track is **not gated**. It comes **before** the leftovers and the battery's
war. It stays inside the gameplay-level decision below: damage is a chance drawn per burst, not a physics model.

**What is there today** (2 Nov 2026):
- An aircraft is alive or destroyed. Every defence decides a kill once per pass:
  - the testbed's neighbours by `WavePassKillChance` (air_war/test_waves.hpp);
  - the air war by `PassKillChance` (air_war/planner.hpp);
  - our Osa by the outcome resolver (phase 3, 6.7).
- The flak's bursts are scattered in Unreal alone (`SimFxAudio.cpp`, `effects.json`): a fixed ±35 m about the aim and a
  ±0.12 s fuze jitter, the same at every range. The burst picture and the kill are drawn independently.
- An aircraft is a point that turns at a fixed rate at constant speed (`aircraft.hpp`). Unreal turns the model to its
  velocity: it never banks, and it never pitches except along its path.
- Evasion (`target_behaviour.hpp`): only when our Osa launches at the aircraft or locks it with a warning receiver
  aboard. The pilot never reacts to the other sites, to flak, or to what he sees. Nothing is jettisoned.
- Ground vehicles are put on the ground by a trace from 20 m above their sim position down to 50 m below it
  (`ASimEntityActor::GroundBelow`). Aircraft are drawn at their sim height without a check.
- The time of day (before R7):
  - simcore had a scenario's `environment.daylight` (true or false) and the air war's `start_hour`;
  - Unreal's sky had its own `LocalTimeHours` (10.5 by default);
  - nothing joined the two, and the menu offered neither. R7 made them one sun (`docs/LIGHT.md`).

## Decision: how far the physics goes (resolved)

The user chose the **gameplay-level route** (phase 3 option 1, `e5271e7`): operators win or lose by how well they run
the procedures, track and engage, and the resolver turns that into outcomes with tunable numbers in `data/gameplay/`.
Hit chances are calibrated to published single-shot ranges (phase 6.7); detailed weapon-performance physics is not
scheduled.
